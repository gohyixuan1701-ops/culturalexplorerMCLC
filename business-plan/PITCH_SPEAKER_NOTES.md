## 1. A doorway to literary research

We are Cultural Explorer. Our Malaysian Chinese Literature Center prototype helps people discover Malaysian Chinese literature through a searchable catalogue and an optional 3D exhibition. Our focus is Chinese studies and research: helping users find a relevant work, inspect its context and follow its source. The current demos show discovery; the membership service is our proposed next stage.

## 2. Research starts with finding the right material

A research task often begins with identifying a work or author, checking edition details and deciding which original materials to consult. We are testing whether a focused catalogue and source links make that first step easier. Users can browse directly; the 3D gallery is an optional introduction. We do not claim to replace a research library or provide every original text.

## 3. Who it is for

Our audience includes Chinese Studies undergraduates, lecturers, postgraduate students, scholars and anyone needing relevant literary documents for research. An undergraduate may be finding assignment material; a lecturer may be checking references; a postgraduate may be exploring an author or translation. These use cases need observation with actual users. Our commercial direction is individual membership, rather than a school purchasing model.

## 4. Existing survey feedback

The existing pitch charts show 75% requesting book excerpts. For subscription intent, 50% say probably, 25% are unsure and 25% probably not. These are exploratory chart values, not observed purchases. We still need the exact questions, sample size, dates and respondent roles from the unchanged Form before making stronger claims. Do not infer a respondent count from percentage increments.

## 5. Feedback changes the roadmap

The strongest visible request is for deeper reading content. Our next step is to seek permissions for useful literary materials and excerpts, then test them in real research tasks. We want to know whether users find relevant sources, return to the collection and see sufficient value in membership. A request for excerpts is a content priority, not proof that a paid service will succeed.

## 6. Two ways into the same literature

The catalogue contains 38 edition records: 28 Centre records and 10 translations. The standalone gallery exhibits 20 selected records. Users can inspect summaries, metadata and sources, and use author information and local bookmarks in the catalogue. Both interfaces support Chinese and English, but English content coverage is partial. There are no complete literary texts or operational membership permissions yet.

## 7. Live demonstration

Open the catalogue, search for a title, inspect its publication information and follow its source. Then open the standalone gallery, search for an exhibited work and inspect its cover and summary. Show an English translation record if useful. Gallery links do not deep-link every catalogue title to a specific exhibit. Keep the two demo URLs available and test the QR codes and venue connection before presenting.

## 8. Positioning

Our position combines a focus on Malaysian Chinese literature, a choice between direct catalogue browsing and an exhibition, and book-specific context with source links. We are not claiming that 3D exhibitions are new or that other libraries lack useful discovery features. Research usefulness depends on coverage, source quality and document access. That value still needs comparison and testing.

## 9. Membership model

The intended model has two content levels. Free members can use only the material designated as free-level content. Paid members can use all content offered on the platform. The exact free selection, approved content collection, fee and billing terms are not yet fixed. The current public demos do not enforce this model: accounts, payment verification and server-side content permissions must be implemented before paid operation.

## 10. Market method

Our market should be sized around relevant people, not school counts. We need a sourced inventory of Chinese Studies programmes, research communities and other relevant users, with duplicate roles removed. TAM describes the relevant population in a defined geography, SAM those we can serve and reach, and SOM the members we can plausibly acquire and retain. We do not yet publish a numerical market value.

## 11. Validation and acquisition

Start by observing title-finding, source-checking and document-discovery tasks with undergraduates, lecturers and postgraduates. Define the free collection and secure a useful paid content offer. Then pilot that offer and test price, paid conversion and renewal when the membership system is ready. Programme communities, research associations and literature groups are potential channels, not confirmed partners.

## 15. Membership economics appendix

Content and rights, hosting and support, payment and outreach all need explicit budgets. The revised calculator intentionally produces no numerical forecast by default. Any scenario inputs are assumptions unless backed by actual operating evidence.
